Overview

Curating a Spring Festival cultural market.

The project explores how a Spring Festival market can become a participatory cultural experience, bringing Asian heritage culture to life through workshops, craft-making, selling stalls, performances and shared learning.

My role
Initiator, lead planner and organiser, responsible for event concept, cultural curation, visual direction, participant coordination, workshop planning and on-site delivery.
Research base
Cultural research into Spring Festival and Asian heritage practices, alongside conversations with makers, performers and participants to shape an engaging public programme.
Outcome
A participatory cultural market integrating workshops, craft experiences, performances, selling stalls and learning materials into one shared community event.

From Celebration to Participation

Turning Spring Festival into a living cultural platform.

I initiated The Breath of Culture to turn Spring Festival from a symbolic celebration into a participatory cultural platform.

In an overseas context, Asian heritage culture is often encountered through festivals or visual symbols. This event invited visitors to experience culture more directly through making, buying, tasting, watching and exchanging stories.

By combining workshops, craft experiences, performances, market stalls and learning materials, the project created a shared space where heritage could be felt, practised and passed on.

Why This Event

Creating a shared festive space that had not existed before.

Before The Breath of Culture, there had not been a Spring Festival market of this kind: a cultural event combining workshops, craft experiences, performances, learning and market stalls.

It became RCA's first Spring Festival cultural market, creating a shared platform for art, design, culture and community gathering.

The event was created to offer overseas Chinese students a moment of gathering and homecoming, while giving visitors unfamiliar with Eastern culture an accessible way to understand it through participation rather than observation.

Curatorial Framework

Each activity was selected through five curatorial lenses.

Each activity was selected through five curatorial lenses: material, technique, symbol, ritual and meaning. This helped transform cultural heritage from static information into a tactile and social experience.

Curated Experience Each activity was curated through material, technique, symbol, ritual and meaning, turning heritage into something visitors could touch, practice, interpret and remember.

Concept

Walking into a living Nianhua.

Nianhua was chosen as the central curatorial system because it is one of the most public and accessible visual languages of Chinese New Year. It naturally appears on doors, walls and streets, carrying blessings, protection, celebration and everyday imagination.

I used the structure of Chinese New Year pictures as a curatorial framework. Instead of treating the image as something to look at, I translated its visual and narrative layers into a walkable market journey, where visitors could enter, make, watch, exchange and leave with their own cultural memory.

Spatial Curation

Organising the courtyard as a walkable Nianhua journey.

The courtyard was organised as a walkable cultural journey. I divided the space into identity, workshop, market, discovery and resting zones, balancing dense browsing areas with slower moments for making, watching and gathering.

By including a resting and social area, the market became not only a place to browse, but also a place to gather, talk, wait, share and stay.

Entry & Identity PointIntroduced the event identity, visual language and take-away materials.
Hands-on Workshop ZoneHosted slower activities such as bamboo weaving, fish lantern making and tea culture performance.
Main Market SpineConnected key stalls into a clear browsing route.
Peripheral Discovery ZoneExtended the market around the courtyard with smaller-scale stalls.
Resting & Social AreaCreated a place for conversation, waiting, reflection and community gathering.

Visual System

A hybrid New Year visual language.

The visual identity used Nianhua as its core reference, translating its bold outlines, symmetrical figures and festive colours into a contemporary event language.

Illustration

The illustration system reimagined traditional New Year print figures through the context of art, design and culture. Weapons and ritual objects were replaced with brushes and pencils, turning guardian-like deities into playful protectors of artists, designers and cultural makers.

Eastern and Western figures are placed within the same folk-print system, using bold black outlines, dense decorative details and red-envelope framing to turn each figure into a contemporary blessing.

Posture

Inspired by Spring Festival couplets.

The main poster was inspired by Spring Festival couplets, an essential visual element of Chinese New Year. Its symmetrical structure echoes the way couplets and door gods frame an entrance, carrying blessings, protection and celebration.

By placing the illustrated figures on both sides of the composition, the poster becomes a symbolic doorway, inviting visitors to step into The Breath of Culture as if entering a festive space shaped by Nianhua, calligraphy and shared cultural memory.

Red Envelope Blessings

Portable blessings visitors could take away.

The red envelope series turned the East-West deity illustrations into portable blessings. Each envelope pairs a mythological figure with a playful contemporary wish, combining Chinese New Year customs with an international art, design and cultural context.

As both a festive object and a take-away design, the red envelopes allowed visitors to carry part of the event's visual system beyond the market.

From Visual System to Lived Experience

The designed framework became a real social space.

The event brought the visual and curatorial system into a real social space. Visitors moved between stalls, workshops, performances and resting areas, encountering heritage through making, watching, buying, talking and staying.

The place design focuses on atmosphere, interaction, craft details and the ways visitors move through and use the space, showing how the market shifted from a designed framework into a lived cultural experience.
