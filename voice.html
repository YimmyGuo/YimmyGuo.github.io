Overview

Designing support for the moments when newcomers have to act.

The project reframes scam prevention as a transition problem. New international students must make housing, payment, banking and support decisions while learning an unfamiliar system.

My role
User research, journey mapping, service strategy, game mechanics and testing synthesis.
Research base
50+ international students, 30 parent interviews, 4 university stakeholders and 45+ testing participants.
Outcome
Online game, newcomer website, info card, board game, scam library and connected support route.

Research

Support exists, but students often miss it at critical decision points.

We mapped how students, parents and university teams experience the first month in the UK, then translated recurring tensions into core insights.

Current Journey & Service Gap

Insights

Insight 1

Unfamiliar Systems

Students often do not know what a normal UK process looks like, so suspicious requests can feel believable.

Insight 2

Guidance Is Hard to Use

Official information exists, but it is often scattered, formal and difficult to use when students need to act.

Insight 3

Pressure Delays Help-Seeking

Under pressure, students may delay asking for help because they feel uncertain, embarrassed or afraid.

Strategy

How can a little triangle learn to round its edges?

Traditional systems explain how the circle works, then expect little triangles to trim themselves to fit. This project takes a more inclusive path: helping them grow a wider circle around who they already are.

When someone grows up in a triangular environment, their habits and behaviours are also shaped by that world. Entering a circular environment can create friction, not because either shape is better, but because the fit has changed.

How can a little triangle learn to round its edges, without cutting away who they already are?

Traditional support systems often explain how the circular environment works, then leave the little triangles to trim themselves into shape so they can fit inside it. Our approach is more inclusive: instead of asking them to cut away their edges, we help them grow a wider circle around who they already are.

Board Game

From individual caution to peer conversation.

A role-play game where players take on different characters, manage money, compare housing options and discuss suspicious moments together. The physical format makes risk easier to talk about in a group.
